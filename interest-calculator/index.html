Simple Interest vs Compound Interest Explained

Understanding how interest accrues is crucial for making smart financial investments or taking loans:

  • Simple Interest (SI): Interest is earned only on the initial principal amount throughout the tenure. It remains constant each year.
  • Compound Interest (CI): Interest is calculated on the initial principal plus the accumulated interest from previous periods ("interest on interest").

Mathematical Formulas

Simple Interest Formula: SI = (P × R × T) / 100

Compound Interest Formula: A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t)

Where P is Principal, R is Rate, T is Time in years, and n is the compounding frequency per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Simple interest calculates return purely on the principal amount, while compound interest adds earned interest back to the principal, generating higher returns over time.

The more frequently interest is compounded (e.g., monthly vs annually), the faster your money grows, resulting in a higher total maturity value.

Most commercial banks in India (such as SBI, HDFC, and ICICI) compound interest on a quarterly basis for standard Fixed Deposits.

The Rule of 72 is a quick shortcut to estimate how many years it will take to double your investment. Simply divide 72 by your expected annual interest rate (e.g., 72 / 8% = 9 years).
