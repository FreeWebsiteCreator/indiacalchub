How loan EMI is calculated

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment — the fixed amount you repay every month toward a loan until it is fully cleared. Each instalment combines a portion of principal and a portion of interest; early in the loan, the interest share is larger, and it shrinks over time as the outstanding principal reduces.

The calculator uses the standard reducing-balance formula:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n / [(1 + r)n − 1]

Here P is the loan amount (principal), r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12 and by 100), and n is the number of monthly instalments (tenure in years multiplied by 12). This is the same method banks and NBFCs use for home loans, car loans, education loans and personal loans in India.

What changes your EMI

  • Interest rate: a lower rate reduces both your EMI and total interest paid.
  • Tenure: a longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI but increases total interest paid over the loan.
  • Prepayment: paying extra toward the principal, even occasionally, shortens the loan and cuts interest cost significantly.

Frequently asked questions

What is EMI in a loan?

EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is the fixed monthly payment you make to repay a loan, made up of a principal component and an interest component, until the loan is fully repaid.

What formula does this EMI calculator use?

It uses the standard reducing-balance formula EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / [(1+r)^n − 1], where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the number of monthly instalments. This matches the method most Indian banks and NBFCs use.

Does prepaying a loan reduce my EMI or my tenure?

It depends on what you choose with your lender. Most banks let you either reduce the tenure (keep EMI the same, finish earlier) or reduce the EMI (keep tenure the same, pay less each month). Reducing tenure usually saves more total interest.

Can I use this calculator for a car loan or personal loan, not just a home loan?

Yes. The EMI formula is identical for home loans, car loans, personal loans and education loans — only the amount, rate and tenure change.
