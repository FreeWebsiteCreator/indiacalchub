What is a Lumpsum Investment?

A Lumpsum Investment is a method where an investor deposits a bulk amount of capital into a mutual fund scheme in one single transaction, as opposed to monthly installments (SIP).

Lumpsum Compound Interest Formula

Future returns on a lumpsum deposit are calculated using the compound interest formula:

A = P × (1 + r/100)^n

Where A is the estimated maturity amount, P is the initial lumpsum invested, r is the annual interest rate, and n is time in years.

Frequently Asked Questions

In SIP, you invest a fixed sum at regular intervals (e.g., monthly), whereas in Lumpsum investment, you invest the full amount at once in a single transaction.

Lumpsum investments are ideal when market valuations are low (market dips/corrections) or when you receive a sudden windfall such as an annual bonus or property sale proceeds.

Most open-ended equity and debt funds have no lock-in period (though exit load may apply if redeemed within 1 year). However, ELSS tax-saving funds have a mandatory 3-year lock-in.

Most Indian mutual fund schemes accept initial lumpsum investments starting from ₹1,000 to ₹5,000.
